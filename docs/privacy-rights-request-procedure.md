# Privacy-rights request procedure

This procedure supports Ruzawi School's handling of requests under Zimbabwe's Cyber and Data Protection Act [Chapter 12:07]. It must be reviewed by the appointed Data Protection Officer (DPO) and Zimbabwean legal counsel and updated when the law, regulator guidance, school systems or suppliers change.

## 1. Ownership and access

- `privacy@ruzawi.com` must be a working, monitored mailbox or alias controlled by the DPO/privacy team.
- Access must be limited to authorised personnel, protected with multi-factor authentication and reviewed periodically.
- At least one trained alternate must monitor the mailbox during absence.
- Privacy requests received elsewhere must be forwarded promptly to `privacy@ruzawi.com` without creating unnecessary copies.

## 2. Register every request

Record the following in a restricted request register:

- the website reference, date and source;
- requester and data subject;
- whether the requester acts for self, a child or another person;
- request types and scope;
- identity/authority verification status and method, without retaining more proof than necessary;
- systems, departments and processors searched;
- decisions, reasons, exemptions or retention grounds;
- communications, completion date and person approving the response; and
- any complaint, appeal or POTRAZ involvement.

Never place identity-document copies in an ordinary email thread or unrestricted spreadsheet.

## 3. Acknowledge and clarify

- The website sends an immediate acknowledgement and reference when email delivery succeeds.
- If a request arrives by another route, acknowledge it and assign a reference.
- Clarify an unclear or very broad request promptly. Help the person identify the information, processing activity, school area and relevant period.
- Do not discourage a valid request or require information unrelated to verification or fulfilment.

## 4. Verify identity and authority proportionately

- Match against information already held where this is sufficient.
- Ask only for additional evidence reasonably needed to prevent disclosure or alteration of another person's data.
- For a child, verify that the requester is a parent or legal guardian entitled to exercise the child's rights.
- For another adult, verify written authority or other lawful representation.
- Provide an approved secure method if documentary proof is necessary. Do not invite identity documents through ordinary email.
- Record the verification result and delete verification copies when they are no longer necessary.

## 5. Identify the applicable right

Assess whether the person asks to:

- be informed about use of their information;
- access information held about them;
- object to all or part of processing;
- correct false, misleading or outdated information;
- delete false or misleading information;
- withdraw consent;
- stop direct marketing; or
- raise a concern about handling, security, recipients or overseas transfers.

Do not treat every deletion request as an instruction to erase immediately. Determine whether information is inaccurate, consent has been withdrawn, an objection is valid, the purpose has ended, or another lawful retention ground still applies.

## 6. Search and preserve scope

- Send scoped search instructions only to departments and processors reasonably likely to hold the information.
- Typical locations include admissions, pupil records, safeguarding/pastoral records, medical records, finance, HR/recruitment, email, website forms, alumni records, backups and relevant service providers.
- Preserve information needed to answer the request while it is under review.
- Keep a record of locations searched and responses received.

## 7. Review third-party and protected information

- Confirm that results concern the verified data subject.
- Correct or remove false and misleading information without undue delay where required.
- Do not disclose another person's information without their express written consent or another lawful basis.
- Redact third-party information where appropriate.
- Consult the DPO or legal counsel before withholding information, relying on an exemption, or refusing all or part of a request.

## 8. Decide and implement

- Record the decision, legal basis and approver.
- Apply approved corrections, objections, consent withdrawals, marketing suppression or deletions across relevant live systems and instruct processors where required.
- Where information must be retained, restrict it to the continuing purpose where appropriate and explain the reason.
- Ensure a marketing objection is added to a suppression list rather than deleting the minimum information needed to honour the objection.
- Verify completion with the responsible departments and processors.

## 9. Respond securely

- Respond without undue delay and within any period required by current Zimbabwean law, regulations, regulator direction or an approved school policy.
- Use a secure delivery method appropriate to the sensitivity and volume of information. Confirm the destination before disclosure.
- Explain what was done, what was not done and why, any continuing retention ground, and how to raise a concern with the DPO or POTRAZ.
- Provide information in an accessible and intelligible form.

## 10. Close, retain and review

- Update the register with the final response and completion date.
- Retain the request record only as long as necessary to demonstrate compliance, administer follow-up and resolve disputes under the school's approved retention schedule.
- Delete working copies and unnecessary identity evidence securely.
- Review recurring issues for required corrections, training, supplier changes, data minimisation or a data-protection impact assessment.

## 11. Escalation

Escalate immediately to the DPO when a request reveals:

- a possible personal-data breach or unauthorised disclosure;
- risk to a child or safeguarding concern;
- disputed identity or authority;
- likely litigation, regulatory action or a POTRAZ complaint;
- information involving several people that cannot readily be separated; or
- processing or an overseas transfer that may lack a valid legal basis or safeguard.

A suspected personal-data breach must be handled under the school's incident procedure, including the statutory notification requirements; it must not wait for the privacy-request process to finish.
